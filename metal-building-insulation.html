Metal garages & steel buildings

A steel shell is a condensation machine. Until it isn’t.

Metal garages are the latest thing for owners who want to invest in their own property — for the value and for the enjoyment. Closed-cell foam is what turns that shell into a space you can actually use.

Why closed-cell

Foam sticks to steel. Batts never could.

Bare metal panel is the coldest surface in the building. Warm interior air hits it, hits the dew point, and the ceiling starts to rain on whatever is parked below. Fiberglass hung behind the ribs does nothing about it — air still reaches the panel, and now the batt is wet too.

Closed-cell foam is sprayed directly to the panel. It bonds to the steel, follows every rib and purlin, seals every seam and lap, and puts an unbroken vapour-resistant layer between your air and that cold surface. There is no cavity for air to reach, so there is nothing to condense on.

  • Stops condensation at the sourceNo dripping ceilings, no rust bloom on tools and equipment.
  • Highest R-value per inchMatters when every inch of headroom and door clearance counts.
  • Adds rigidity to the panelClosed-cell stiffens the assembly it bonds to, and quiets rain drum.
  • Makes the space conditionableA mini-split can finally hold a temperature in there.

How we work on site

The prep is the job

Spray foam is unforgiving of bad masking. Overspray does not wipe off — so we spend the time up front instead of apologising afterwards.

  1. Walk & plan

    We confirm thickness, product and sequence with you before anything is covered.

  2. Mask everything

    Doors, tracks, lifts, windows, vehicles, electrical and any exposed studs you want left clean.

  3. Spray in lifts

    Controlled passes to the specified thickness, worked panel line by panel line.

  4. Trim & strip

    Foam trimmed where it needs to be flush, masking pulled, offcuts bagged.

  5. Final walk

    We hand the building back and go over coverage with you before we leave.
